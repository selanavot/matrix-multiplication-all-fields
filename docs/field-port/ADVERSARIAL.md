## Claim assessed

For every field F, in an arbitrary universe, the theorem concludes `OAI.MatrixMultiplication.Arithmetic.omega F ≤ 9/4` using the original arithmetic-program model. The supplied positive-slack consequence states that for each ε > 0 there is C > 0, chosen before the matrix size n, such that every positive n has a correct finite straight-line program of cost at most `C * n^(9/4 + ε)`. The constants and programs may depend on F and ε. A separate theorem bounds the exact coefficient-tensor rank exponent by 9/4.

The model counts scalar field addition, subtraction, and multiplication at unit cost and gives free input/constant loads. The result is an existential, nonuniform arithmetic-complexity bound. It does not supply an executable uniform algorithm, practical constants, bit-complexity bounds, a field-independent C, or a zero-slack `O(n^(9/4))` program bound.

## Why the main attempted counterexamples failed

**Finite fields and imperfect fields.** Fourier periods are selected as `5*M` or `5*M+1` so their field casts are nonzero, with a uniform real bound `6*M`. Interpolation uses distinct nonzero nodes in the algebraic closure, rather than natural-number casts assumed distinct. This covers characteristics 2, 3, and 5. Descent uses an F-linear coordinate functional taking 1 to 1; it does not require a nonzero trace or separability.

**A moving extension degree.** The proof first fixes one finite rank decomposition and the finite algebra generated by its coefficients, then quantifies over all tensor powers. The same dimension d gives the rank cost `d² * R^j` for every j. That fixed overhead disappears before taking the infimum over block sizes. The algebra cannot silently grow with the tensor-power parameter.

**Weakened tensor semantics or missing arithmetic costs.** Rank and restriction remain exact coefficient identities. Natural numbers in the tensor semiring mean that many independent diagonal summands, so p summands do not become zero in characteristic p. Characters are obtained from the retained restriction order and real-valued state construction. The arithmetic bridge builds actual programs and charges the linear-combination overhead `6*R*b²*m²`, in addition to recursive multiplications. Positive ε absorbs the boundary recurrence cost; padding covers all positive sizes. The infimum argument does not assume the optimum is attained.

**Characteristic-sensitive numerical generalization.** The additional review checked the modified determinant, sector, entropy, and profile interfaces against the baseline. It found no hidden division by 2, 3, or factorials in the scalar field, no unsupported simultaneous coordinate change, and no loss of the Fourier factor six in tensor amplification. The numerical fractions and entropy estimates operate over the reals. This was a targeted review of the changed argument and interfaces, not an independent reconstruction of every unchanged numerical inequality.

## Confirmed finding and interpretation boundary

The concrete source defect found was a stale comment in `AuxiliarySeparation/Entropy/Tag.lean` describing a fixed loss of five. The actual statement and proof already used six. The coordinator corrected the comment on the audit branch; no mathematical change was needed.

Over finite fields, upstream `MatrixAlgorithm.Correct` means correctness on field-valued inputs, not equality of formal polynomials. For example, `x*x*y` computes `x*y` on F₂ but is a different formal polynomial. This inherited distinction does not invalidate the assessed upper bound: the proof establishes the stronger exact coefficient-tensor rank bound before constructing programs, and those coefficient identities survive scalar homomorphisms. A separate formal symbolic-program predicate and equivalence between exponent definitions have not been established here.
